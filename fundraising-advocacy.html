01

Fundraising · Hunger relief · Direct response

Carmei Hair

A dignity-first fundraising treatment built around one intimate human story, then widened into the full scope of the organization’s daily work.

Challenge
Make hunger in Jerusalem immediate without turning the people served into abstractions.
Approach
Open with Avigail’s story; move from crisis to welcome, nourishment, community, and a specific invitation to keep the doors open.
Strength shown
Narrative sequencing, donor empathy, benefit-led copy, and visual pacing across a four-page appeal.

Fundraising · Children & families · Visual narrative

Tikvah Layeled

A compassionate campaign concept designed to translate a child’s medical struggle into a clear, emotionally paced story of practical hope.

Approach
Use sequential storytelling to make the need understandable at a glance and connect donor action to relief, continuity, and possibility.
Strength shown
Cause framing, reader guidance, sensitive tone, and the use of images as part of the argument—not decoration.

04

Advocacy · Public-interest messaging · Visual concept

Refuse to Serve

An advocacy treatment that turns a charged position into a disciplined piece of communication: immediate headline, unmistakable stance, and a visual built for recall.

Challenge
Create attention without letting provocation overwhelm the message or the audience’s path through it.
Approach
Lead with the decisive phrase, use editorial tension and protest-poster language, then frame the idea as a question of conscience and choice.
Strength shown
Advocacy voice, headline craft, bold art direction, and message control for a polarizing subject.

The craft behind the work.

Find the human center

Begin with a person, choice, or moment the audience can immediately understand.

Make the message move

Sequence information so feeling leads naturally to understanding—and understanding to action.

Design for response

Use hierarchy, contrast, pacing, and a clear ask to make the next step feel obvious.
